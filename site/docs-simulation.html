Every hit has a path

A duel on the frozen lake: a T-80U takes the hit

The battle simulation advances at 60 Hz. Movement, aim, ballistics, armor, damage, reloads, spotting, and match results are resolved from authoritative state—not from the rendered frame.

9 min

Fixed-step battle loop

The authoritative step uses metres, seconds, radians, and a fixed 1/60-second interval. Input is sampled into explicit vehicle controls before movement, combat, spotting, and result evaluation run in a stable order. Render rate can change without changing the number or order of simulation steps.

Frame-time spikes accumulate into bounded fixed steps. Authoritative randomness is seeded or injected. Wall-clock time and Math.random() are excluded from rules that affect a shot, reload, module state, or match result.

Aim and shot creation

Camera aim and gun aim are separate. The camera chooses the requested point; traverse, elevation, suspension attitude, and bore obstruction determine where the barrel can actually point. A shot starts at the resolved muzzle transform with the current shell, velocity, dispersion, and owner identity.

The HUD draws both states. The camera marker communicates the request. The gun marker communicates the ballistic line. “Path blocked” and gun-limit feedback therefore describe physical constraints instead of repainting the request as truth.

Armor trace and penetration

The shell segment is tested against world collision before vehicle armor. Vehicle queries transform the ray into the target pose, order plate crossings, calculate distance and angle, apply normalization or ricochet rules, and consume the shell’s remaining penetration. Spaced layers and internal volumes stay in traversal order.

A penetration continues through internal module and crew volumes. A non-penetration still produces an authoritative impact event. Presentation receives the resolved path and result; it does not rerun the decision.

Damage, reloads, and special weapons

Damage updates hit points, tracks, engine, fuel, ammunition, gun, turret drive, optics, and crew state. Autoloaders distinguish intra-clip delay from magazine reload. Guided missiles retain a live projectile and steering state until impact or expiry. Fires, ammunition-rack events, ramming, and repairs use the same event boundary.

The X-ray replay, hit card, damage log, reload rack, impact effects, and sound all consume those events. This keeps visible feedback aligned with the result used by solo and multiplayer authority.

Battle rules

Standard Battle, Capture the Flag, Zone Control, Turbo Ball, and Endless Horde compose over the same complete tank simulation. Flag and zone modes add six-second respawns. Turbo Ball keeps weapons active while tanks drive at 1.85× mobility and shells can strike the ball. Horde escalates deterministic waves of fragile bots and places repair caches.

The renderer-free mode controller owns scores, objectives, respawn timers, wave state, caches, and bot objective points. Solo, private rooms, and LAN rooms use the same rules; the host choice travels in canonical lobby state and survives rematches.

Bosses, infection, critical damage and weapon ladders

Juggernaut lets you play the boss or join the hunters. The boss is 12% larger, has a pulsing shield aura, eight times its hull health and half its reload time. Hunters respawn; the boss must survive ten minutes. Infected starts with five infected (at least three in custom battles). Survivors have 130% health; infected have 20%, 30% more speed and their own reload, and hunt the survivors as a pack, returning five seconds after every death. Destroyed survivors join the infected. Survive three minutes, or convert everyone. Both modes play with unlimited rounds.

Realistic uses normal spotting and removes hull HP attrition. Penetrations reliably injure crew, apply 2.5× module damage and spread fragments through the struck compartments. Fires damage modules four times faster. Ammunition destruction, fewer than two crew or a destroyed engine and gun eliminate the tank. Gun Game advances through five weapons with two kills per stage; respawns preserve progress.

Direct drone flight and the AC-130 scope

In Drone mode, press V or tap the drone control to launch a quadcopter from your tank. After the launch animation you directly pilot its first-person view. W/S move, A/D strafe, Space climbs and mouse or touch look steers; pitch down and fly forward to dive. The battery lasts 40 seconds within an 850 m radius. Fly into an enemy to deliver an explosive impact. Once you are flying its feed, V returns you to your parked, vulnerable tank: the drone flies home, lands on its dock and is ready again 5 seconds after landing; after an impact, an empty battery or a lost link the next drone takes 25 seconds. V does nothing during the launch. National drones have detailed service airframes, camera gimbals and payloads. Large turrets carry the launch rail; compact turrets may use the hull. Impact, battery expiry and a lost link trigger a short static transition; a return closes the feed cleanly. The minimap tracks the drone separately from its carrier. I or the sensor button cycles white-hot infrared, colored thermal, green night vision and daylight; the same options are available in tank scopes. Heat highlights visible tanks and bright explosions, without bypassing cover or spotting.

The AC-130 follows an automatic orbit with an overhead aiming scope. Mouse or touch controls aim; the wheel zooms. Select the 30 mm autocannon, 152 mm explosive howitzer or guided missiles with 1–3 or the ammunition buttons. These have independent reload channels and unlimited ammunition. Shots and drone impacts use the same host-authoritative collision, armor and damage rules as tank weapons. Protect the vulnerable allied ground convoy: escorts have 90% hull strength and must physically reach the green E extraction marker. Save at least half before the eight-minute deadline; losing too many allies fails the mission. The objective tracks surviving troops, rescued troops and route progress. In multiplayer every pilot supplies air support while separate allied bots drive the convoy. Enemies cannot see or shoot the aircraft. Howitzer blasts reach 22 m and missile blasts reach 18 m. J drops ammunition and K drops medical supplies at the ground aim point; icons support touch input. Crates land after six seconds. Nearby allies that need them detour to collect them. Ammo restores finite ground reserves; medicine repairs 35% of maximum health. Cooldowns are 22/30 seconds and crates expire after 70 seconds. The minimap shows the aircraft, orbit, ground aim, extraction and supplies.

Verification

Node-runnable self-tests cover movement, combat, spotting, missile guidance, special actions, AI aim, and the complete authoritative match. Browser probes add bore parity, projectile travel, live impact effects, and HUD alignment.

Run node src/sim/combat.selftest.mjs, node src/sim/authoritativeMatch.selftest.mjs, and npm test after changing shared combat rules.

Standard Battle and team setup

Standard Battle is elimination: defeat the opposing team before the fifteen-minute clock ends. Destroyed vehicles do not respawn. The scoreboard carries the essential information, so this mode has no extra objective banner. Choose 7 v 7, 14 v 14 or custom teams before launch. In custom solo battles, the player counts as an ally; the field limit is 42 vehicles and allied bots yield when needed to preserve the requested enemy count.

Battle Setup stores settings for each mode. Team presets, enemy nation and eligible times of day are chosen before starting. The Battle button launches the configured game; Multiplayer has its own entry for creating or joining a room without accidentally starting a solo match.

Capture the Flag

Take the enemy flag back to your own base while protecting your home flag. The default target is three captures, with a fifteen-minute clock and six-second respawns; setup can change the score and respawn settings. A flag carrier keeps its full speed. Recovering a dropped friendly flag and escorting a carrier can be more valuable than chasing a distant kill.

Use fast vehicles for the run, keep a defender near home and cover the carrier’s return lane. Bots assign carrier, recovery, escort, raid and defense roles rather than sending the entire team to one flag. Destroying an enemy opens a route, but a kill is not a flag capture.

Zone Control and Gravity Mode

Zone Control rewards taking and holding three sectors. The default target is 750 points, with six-second respawns and a fifteen-minute clock. Destroying an opposing tank adds 25 points in Zone Control and Gravity Mode. Spread pressure across the map: leaving every sector to pursue one damaged tank gives the enemy time to score.

Gravity Mode uses the zone objective with a twelve-minute clock, rocket jumps, boosted mobility and selectable gravity. Mars is 0.38 g, Moon 0.17 g and Earth 1 g. Cache frequency is configurable. Its default vehicle modifiers are 1.25× speed, 1.2× hull health, 0.9× damage and 0.9× reload time. Recoil and airborne motion matter: choose a landing zone before launching.

Turbo Ball

Drive or shoot the ball into the enemy goal. The default match lasts ten minutes, ends at five goals and uses three-second respawns. Vehicles have 0.6 g gravity, 1.85× speed, 1.5× hull health, half damage and 0.7× reload time. Ammunition is unlimited; equipment, consumables and critical module damage are disabled for this mode.

Get behind the ball, line up the goal and commit to a controlled push. Jumping and amplified recoil enable aerial plays but can overshoot the ball. A striker, supporting screens and a goal defender make a stronger team than several tanks wedged around the same contact point. Destroying tanks disrupts the opposition; goals decide the score.

Endless Horde and Frontline Assault

Endless Horde has no match clock or respawns, and rounds are unlimited. Your side has twice its hull health; attackers arrive at 15% of theirs, 2% tougher each wave. Survive waves that grow from six attackers by two each wave, with an additional surge every third wave under the default settings. Clearing a wave repairs 30% of maximum hull health; use the floating repair caches to sustain the run. Setup can change the allied support, hostile identity pool, opening wave and enemy nation.

Frontline Assault is a sequential breakthrough: take three sectors against escalating defenders, then hold the final sector for twenty seconds. The default clock is twelve minutes and running out of time is a defeat. Campaign operations vary the battlefield and difficulty; clearing an operation opens the next. Preserve your hull for the final hold rather than treating it like a respawn mode.

Driving, airtime and landing damage

Movement runs at a fixed sixty steps per second. Engine power, weight, forward and reverse limits, track condition, terrain resistance and hull rotation jointly determine handling. The renderer interpolates those states rather than changing movement with frame rate. Handbrake behavior is grounded; landing after a jump must not silently latch the brake.

A fall is a physical impact, not just a height threshold. Mass, impact speed, attitude and mode physics affect the result. Reduced gravity changes both your arc and the terrain you can safely reach. Avoid blind drops at bridge abutments or cliff edges; use a route with a visible landing and enough room to settle.
